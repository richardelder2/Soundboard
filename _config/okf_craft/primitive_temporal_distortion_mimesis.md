# Chronesthesia: The Narrative Time Ratio

Story time ($ST$, the hours/years elapsed in the story world) and Discourse time ($DT$, the seconds/pages the reader takes to read) almost never match. The manipulation of this ratio—**Chronesthesia**—is the primary cognitive dial of narrative pacing.

Gérard Genette proved that there are only **four primitive speeds of time in fiction**:

---

## 1. The Four Primitive Speeds of Time

| Speed | Mathematical Ratio | Cognitive Function | When to Deploy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scene (Real-Time)** | $DT \approx ST$ | The baseline of immersion. Spoken dialogue, active physical blocking, moment-to-moment presence. | Crucial character negotiations, turning points, crisis dilemmas. |
| **Summary (Compression)** | $DT \ll ST$ | Accelerating time. Compressing days, weeks, or years into three sentences. | Routine transit, uneventful recovery periods, bridging narrative hubs. |
| **Stretch / Pause (Dilated Focus)**| $DT \gg ST$ | Slow-motion perception. 2 seconds of physical time dilated across 3 pages of sensory observation and internal triage. | High-velocity crises (gunshot, car skid, explosive realization of betrayal). |
| **Ellipsis (Omission)** | $DT = 0, ST > 0$ | Total temporal jump cut. Zero words given to a span of elapsed time. | Cutting out travelogue, bathroom breaks, and unchallenging routines. |

---

## 2. Invariant Rules for SAGA

*   **Pacing Friction Law:** AI defaults to a uniform, mid-paced stroll. Masterful human fiction constantly swings between **Ellipsis/Summary** (skipping two months in a sentence) and **Dilated Pause** (spending 500 words on the single second an eye twitches).
*   **The Shock Dilatation:** At the climax of physical or emotional shock, immediately shift into Dilated Pause ($DT \gg ST$) to mimic trauma adrenaline.
