# Dwight Swain's MRU Architecture & The Pacing Velocity Equation

In commercial fiction and bestselling narrative engineering, scene momentum is governed by the structural alternation of **Scenes** (action phases) and **Sequels** (processing phases), executed through the granular micro-rhythm of **Motivation-Reaction Units (MRUs)**.

When a manuscript lags, it is almost never a failure of vocabulary; it is a mechanical failure of the **Pacing Velocity Equation**—allowing reflective sequels to dilate during high-stakes external crises.

---

## 1. The Macro-Rhythm: Scene vs. Sequel

Every narrative sequence operates as an alternating binary loop:

### The Scene (Proactive Drive)
1.  **Goal:** A concrete, immediate, measurable objective the protagonist seeks to achieve right now.
2.  **Conflict:** Real-time physical or social obstacles thrown up by the environment or an opponent.
3.  **Disaster:** A sudden, unexpected complication that denies the goal or grants it with a devastating catch ("Yes, but..." or "No, and furthermore...").

### The Sequel (Reactive Processing)
1.  **Reaction:** Visceral, emotional, and cognitive response to the disaster (shock, horror, somatic pain).
2.  **Dilemma:** Evaluation of the dwindling options; every available path forward involves severe risk or loss.
3.  **Decision:** The commitment to a new, urgent goal, which immediately launches the next Scene.

---

## 2. The Granular Engine: Motivation-Reaction Units (MRUs)

At the paragraph and sentence level, human perception processes experience in an unalterable neuro-physiological sequence:

```
[External Stimulus / Motivation]  --->  [1. Visceral Reflex]  --->  [2. Emotional / Cognitive Appraisal]  --->  [3. Action / Speech]
```

### The Invariable Order:
1.  **Motivation (External & Objective):** Something the character sees, hears, or physically feels (e.g., *"The deadbolt clicked."*).
2.  **Reaction 1 (Visceral / Involuntary):** Immediate nervous system response (e.g., *"Her breath snagged in her throat."*).
3.  **Reaction 2 (Emotional / Cognitive):** Rapid psychological appraisal (e.g., *"He wasn't supposed to be home until dawn."*).
4.  **Reaction 3 (Voluntary Action / Speech):** Intentional bodily movement or spoken words (e.g., *"She slipped the kitchen knife into her sleeve and stepped behind the pantry door."*).

*Rule of Craft:* Reversing this sequence (e.g., having a character scream or draw a weapon *before* describing what startled them) destroys reader immersion by violating cognitive processing reality.

---

## 3. The Swain Pacing Velocity Equation

Narrative speed can be modeled mathematically as the ratio of proactive momentum to reflective processing:

$$\text{Pacing Velocity} = \frac{\text{Words of Proactive Action (Scene)}}{\text{Words of Internal Processing (Sequel)}}$$

### Pacing Compression Trajectory Across a Manuscript:

*   **Act I (0% – 25%):**
    *   *Ratio:* ~60% Scene : 40% Sequel.
    *   *Function:* Grants necessary cognitive space to establish the character's internal wound, domestic status quo, and baseline worldbuilding. Chapter lengths: 3,000 – 4,500 words.
*   **Act II (25% – 75%):**
    *   *Ratio:* ~75% Scene : 25% Sequel.
    *   *Function:* Accelerates pressure. Following scene disasters, the character’s reflective Sequels are compressed; internal processing is forced to collapse directly into emergency decisions under acute duress. Chapter lengths: 2,500 – 3,500 words.
*   **Act III (75% – 100%):**
    *   *Ratio:* ~90% Scene : 10% Sequel (or pure continuous Scene).
    *   *Function:* Reflective deliberation is stripped away entirely. The protagonist operates on instinct, muscle memory, and immediate existential choice. Chapter lengths shrink to 1,200 – 1,800 words, terminating on cliffhangers and rapid reversals.
