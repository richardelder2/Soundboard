# Multi-POV Structural Orchestration

Orchestrating multiple point-of-view (POV) characters requires distinct narrative voices, contrasting cognitive filters, and strategic information asymmetry across chapter transitions.

---

## Core Rules for Multi-POV Fiction

1. **Cognitive Filtering:**
   * Two characters in the same room experience different realities. A carpenter notices roof joints and door warps; a thief notices line of sight and pocket bulges. Never write generic observation in a POV scene.
2. **Information Asymmetry (Dramatic Irony):**
   * Use POV shifts to let the reader know a secret that Character B does not yet possess. This transforms passive reading into suspense.
3. **Voice Separation:**
   * Each POV character must have an individual cadence, vocabulary ceiling, and preferred sensory dialect (visual vs. auditory vs. tactile).
4. **Cliffhanger Handoffs:**
   * Cut away from POV A at a moment of decision or surprise; pick up POV B where the emotional stakes are equally high.
