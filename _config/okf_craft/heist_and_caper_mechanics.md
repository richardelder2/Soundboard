# The Heist & Caper Engine

A successful heist story operates as a double-layered narrative: the crew executes a heist against the target, while the author executes a "long con" against the reader.

---

## 1. The Tripartite Pacing Formula (The Explain vs. The Real Plan)

1. **The Briefing (The Mental Map):**
   * The team lays out the plan step-by-step. The reader is given clear expectations of how the operation *should* unfold.
2. **The Inevitable Catastrophe (The Go-Wrong):**
   * Halfway through execution, an unforeseen complication shatters the plan (guards change early, security key fails, vault seal jams). The audience experiences acute crisis.
3. **The Hidden Architecture (The True Plan):**
   * In the climax, the narrative reveals that the "catastrophe" was either deliberately baited or that the crew's real target was completely different from what was stated in Step 1.
   * *The Soderbergh Rule:* Never cheat. Every component of the true escape was shown on page early on, but disguised as comic relief, character bickering, or background prop handling.

---

## 2. Structural Requirements in SAGA

*   **Assembly of Specialists:** Each crew member must possess an idiosyncratic, non-redundant competence and an unresolved personal grudge.
*   **The Ticking Alarm:** The heist must operate within a physical or temporal boundary that progressively narrows (e.g., thermal sensors warming up, police sirens closing distance).
