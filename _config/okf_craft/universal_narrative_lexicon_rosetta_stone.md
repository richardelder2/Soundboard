# The Universal Narrative Rosetta Stone

Writers arrive at the novel engineering table steeped in distinct craft traditions. Some speak fluent **Story Grid** (*5 Commandments, Value Shifts*), others think in **Save the Cat!** (*Dark Night of the Soul, B-Story, Fun & Games*), **The Hero's Journey** (*The Ordeal, Threshold Guardians*), **Dan Harmon's Story Circle**, **K.M. Weiland** (*The Lie Your Character Believes*), **John Truby**, or **Brandon Sanderson's Laws of Magic**.

The **Universal Narrative Rosetta Stone** maps every major school's terms directly to their underlying cognitive primitives and cross-framework equivalents. Agents and editors must **mirror the author's native vocabulary**, recognizing their meaning without forcing them to learn an alien taxonomy.

## 2. School-Specific Lexicons & Diagnostic Aliases

### A. Story Grid Lexicon (Shawn Coyne)
*   **The 5 Commandments of the Scene:** Inciting Incident $\rightarrow$ Progressive Complication $\rightarrow$ Crisis Question $\rightarrow$ Climax $\rightarrow$ Resolution. Codified in [`scene_level_five_commandments_coyne.md`](file:///C:/Users/richa/saga_icm/_config/okf_craft/scene_level_five_commandments_coyne.md).
*   **Crisis Question (Binary Dilemma):** Must be either *Best Bad Choice* (both options bring painful sacrifice) or *Irreconcilable Goods* (two righteous goals that cannot co-exist).
*   **Obligatory Scenes & Conventions:** Non-negotiable genre covenants that reader expectations demand. Codified in `setup/genre_bibles/` and OKF genre structures.
*   **Foolscap Global Story Grid:** 1-page architectural ledger covering global genre, value shifts, controlling idea, and beginning/middle/end hooks. Codified in `stages/02_planning/output/01_macro_arc/foolscap.md`.
*   **Controlling Idea / Theme:** A single sentence linking the causal climax to the philosophical result: *"Life triumphs over death when the hero embraces unconditional self-sacrifice."*

### B. Save the Cat! Lexicon (Blake Snyder / Jessica Brody)
*   **The Logline Formula:** Protagonist + Flaw + Inciting Catalyst + Clear Goal + Concrete Conflict + High Stakes.
*   **The Shard of Glass / The Wound:** The past trauma that established the character’s internal flaw.
*   **The B-Story:** The thematic relationship (mentor, love interest, rival) that carries the philosophical lesson necessary to fix the character's internal wound.
*   **The Pope in the Pool:** Injecting an eccentric, entertaining, or bizarre physical action during a necessary exposition scene to prevent informational boredom.
*   **Save the Cat Beat:** An early, small act of selflessness or competence performed by an otherwise cynical or flawed protagonist to instantly secure audience empathy.
*   **Double Bump:** A climactic set-piece where the apparent victory is overturned by an immediate, unexpected second crisis before final resolution.

### C. Character Arc Theory (K.M. Weiland)
*   **The Lie the Character Believes:** The false psychological premise the protagonist uses as emotional armor at the beginning of the story (e.g., *"I can only be safe if I trust no one"*).
*   **The Want vs. The Need:**
    *   *The Want:* The external, conscious, material goal pursued in Acts I and II.
    *   *The Need:* The internal, unconscious spiritual truth required to heal and achieve wholeness.
*   **The Ghost / Backstory Wound:** The specific historical moment of betrayal or humiliation that convinced the character that the Lie was true.
*   **The Mirror Moment:** The midpoint scene where the protagonist looks in a literal or metaphorical mirror, realizes they are their own greatest obstacle, and begins discarding the Lie.

### D. Tenacious Writing & Modern Author Engine (Sanderson & King)
*   **Sanderson’s First Law of Magic:** An author's ability to solve problems with magic in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic.
*   **Hard Magic vs. Soft Magic:** Explicit, predictable rules with clear costs (Hard) vs. numinous, awe-inspiring, unpredictable power (Soft). Maps to the *Systemic Invariance Contract*.
*   **Promise, Progress, Payoff (Sanderson):** The tripartite contract of narrative momentum: making an exciting promise in Act I, demonstrating visible incremental progress in Act II, and delivering an earned, uncompromised payoff in Act III.
*   **The Box / Closed Door (Stephen King):** The psychological discipline of drafting the first pass with the study door closed—writing raw, instinctive prose without allowing the critical inner editor to tamper with flow.
*   **Head-Hopping (POV Violation):** Accidentally shifting between characters' internal thoughts within the same scene. Maps to *Psychic Distance Whiplash*.

### E. The Anatomy of Story (John Truby)
*   **The 22 Building Blocks:** Truby's plot framework tracing internal moral need to final community restoration.
*   **Psychological Need vs. Moral Need:** Psychological need affects only the self; moral need affects how the character treats others.
*   **Four-Corner Opposition:** Designing an ensemble where Protagonist, Main Opponent, Secondary Rival, and Wildcard compete for the exact same territory under 4 competing moral worldviews. Codified in [`four_corner_opposition_and_foil_matrix.md`](file:///C:/Users/richa/saga_icm/_config/okf_craft/four_corner_opposition_and_foil_matrix.md).

### F. Academic & Formal Narratology (Genette, Bordwell, Ryan)
*   **Fabula vs. Syuzhet:** The chronological, objective raw events (Fabula) vs. the strategic textual arrangement and disclosure on the page (Syuzhet).
*   **Zero, Internal & External Focalization:** Panoramic narrator vs. character's cognitive filter vs. detached unreadable camera. Codified in [`primitive_focalization_filter_levels.md`](file:///C:/Users/richa/saga_icm/_config/okf_craft/primitive_focalization_filter_levels.md).
*   **Free Indirect Discourse (FID):** Third-person narration absorbing character idiom without tags. Codified in [`free_indirect_discourse_and_voice_blending.md`](file:///C:/Users/richa/saga_icm/_config/okf_craft/free_indirect_discourse_and_voice_blending.md).
*   **Paralipsis:** Deliberate lateral withholding within the character's field of vision. Codified in [`irony_typology_and_lateral_omission_paralipsis.md`](file:///C:/Users/richa/saga_icm/_config/okf_craft/irony_typology_and_lateral_omission_paralipsis.md).

## 3. Concierge Rule of Engagement: Vocabulary Mirroring

When conversing with or executing instructions for an author in the ADE:
1.  **Identify the Author's Dialect:** Immediately detect whether the author uses *Story Grid*, *Save the Cat!*, *Hero's Journey*, *Weiland Arcs*, or *Sanderson mechanics*.
2.  **Adopt Their Terminology in Dialogue:** Never correct the author or insist on a different term. If they ask about their "All Hope Is Lost beat", discuss their *All Hope Is Lost beat*.
3.  **Execute the Universal Mechanics Behind the Scenes:** Run the underlying CPOCL plan-threat, Swain velocity equation, and modal discrepancy matrix under the hood, presenting results in the author's preferred language.
