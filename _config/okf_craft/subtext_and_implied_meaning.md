# Dialogue Subtext & The Iceberg Principle

Characters in high-tension drama rarely say what they actually mean. Subtext is the silent, volatile current running beneath mundane surface speech.

---

## 1. The Three Layers of Dialogue

1. **Text (The Spoken Surface):**
   * What the character literally utters ("Can you pass the butter?").
2. **Subtext (The Internal Truth):**
   * What the character actually wants or feels ("You didn't come home last night and I know who you were with").
3. **Context (The Environmental Pressure):**
   * Why they cannot say it aloud (Their children are sitting across the table; the boss is in the room).

---

## 2. Techniques for Engineering Subtext

*   **Displaced Conflict (Fighting Over Props):**
   * Characters argue violently over an unwashed dish, a broken thermostat, or navigation directions because they cannot bear to voice the real wound.
*   **The Incongruous Physical Action:**
   * Pair reassuring words with rejecting body language (saying "I love you" while stepping back and looking out the window).
*   **Strategic Avoidance (The Empty Center):**
   * The most important subject in the room is the one thing neither character will name.
