# The Dual-Track Counterpoint Engine

In modern award-winning fiction and cinema, stories rarely follow a single protagonist in isolation. They deploy **Dual-Track Counterpoint**: two opposing, symmetrical forces moving toward an inevitable collision.

---

## 1. The Symmetry of Antagonism (The Hunter and the Hunted)

*   **Equal Psychological Weight:** The antagonist is not a cardboard obstacle; they receive their own narrative track, internal logic, and philosophical worldview (*Heat*: Vincent Hanna vs. Neil McCauley; *No Country for Old Men*: Llewelyn Moss vs. Anton Chigurh).
*   **The Symmetrical Flaw:** Both characters are trapped in mirror versions of the same obsession.
*   **Cross-Cut Pacing:** Chapters alternate between Track A and Track B. Track A makes a tactical decision; Track B discovers the physical aftermath two hours later. The tension comes from the narrowing temporal and physical distance between them.

---

## 2. Invariant Rules for SAGA Dual-Track

1. **The Shared Crucible Scene:**
   * Before the final battle, the two opposing tracks must share a quiet, non-violent conversation (the diner scene in *Heat*, the hotel phone call in *No Country*). This establishes mutual respect and philosophical clarity before the slaughter.
2. **Asymmetric Revelation:**
   * Track A is always one step ahead in physical movement, while Track B is one step ahead in deductive understanding.
