# The Fragmented Prism Narrative: The Citizen Kane Model

*Citizen Kane* broke cinematic storytelling forever by proving that a human life cannot be understood linearly or through a single authoritative narrator. A life can only be perceived as a **fragmented prism reflected through the biased memories of witnesses**.

---

## 1. The Architectural Structure

*   **The Dying Utterance (The Catalyst / MacGuffin):** Charles Foster Kane dies whispering a single word: *"Rosebud."*
*   **The Inquisitor's Quest:** A reporter (Thompson) is assigned to interview everyone who knew Kane to decode the word. Thompson is not a traditional hero; he is a shadowy silhouette acting as the reader's stand-in.
*   **The Five Witness Perspectives:**
    1.  *Thatcher (The Banker / Legal Guardian):* Views Kane as an ungrateful, reckless socialist child.
    2.  *Bernstein (The Loyal General Manager):* Views Kane as a misunderstood, generous genius.
    3.  *Leland (The Alienated Best Friend):* Views Kane as an empty egotist who wanted love without giving it.
    4.  *Susan Alexander (The Ex-Wife / Opera Singer):* Views Kane as a tyrannical abuser who used her as a pet doll.
    5.  *Raymond (The Cynical Butler):* Views Kane as a pathetic, senile old eccentric throwing tantrums.

---

## 2. Invariant Rules for Prism Fiction in Prose

1. **Contradictory Memory:**
   * The same scene (e.g., Kane's first newspaper editorial or an argument in a tent) should be described differently by different witnesses, with vocabulary reflecting their self-justifications.
2. **The Unrevealed Inquisitor / Unshared Truth:**
   * The reporter never discovers what Rosebud means. He concludes: *"I don't think any word can explain a man's life."*
   * Only the audience/reader is shown the truth in the final image (the sled burning in the incinerator)—proving that Kane’s entire empire was an empty attempt to buy back the childhood love stolen from him.
