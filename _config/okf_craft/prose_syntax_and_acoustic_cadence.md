# Prose Syntax & Acoustic Cadence

Prose is an acoustic medium. Robotic AI prose defaults to mid-length declarative sentences with dead cadence. High-resonance prose operates orchestrally, shifting syntax and phonosemantics to match psychology.

---

## 1. Parataxis vs. Hypotaxis

* **Parataxis (Uncoordinated Juxtaposition):** Linking clauses only with coordinating conjunctions (*"and"*, *"but"*) or commas (Hemingway, McCarthy).
  * *Effect:* Flat fatalism, immediacy, unvarnished physical presence.
  * *Ex:* *"He walked down the stairs and the door was open and rain soaked the carpet and he did not turn back."*
* **Hypotaxis (Hierarchical Subordination):** Nesting dependent clauses beneath a dominant clause (*"although"*, *"because"*, *"inasmuch"*) (Faulkner, James).
  * *Effect:* Intellectual complexity, rumination, moral ambivalence.
  * *Ex:* *"Although he had known the house was condemned, he stepped across the threshold with hesitant reverence, knowing that if his father’s letters remained, his life was built on a forgery."*

---

## 2. Pacing Velocity: Periodic vs. Cumulative

* **Periodic (Suspended Resolution):** Subject and main verb delayed until sentence end.
  * *Effect:* Suspense and forward momentum.
  * *Ex:* *"Past screaming sentries and shattered glass, bleeding from three shrapnel tears, Marcus ran."*
* **Cumulative (Sensory Avalanche):** Base clause upfront followed by cascading modifiers.
  * *Effect:* Sensory immersion, physical exhaustion, awe.
  * *Ex:* *"Marcus ran, dodging through smoke-choked halls, boot soles slipping on oil, breath ragged."*

---

## 3. Gary Provost’s Cadence Rule
Vary sentence length to create music. Short sentences snap. Medium sentences carry narrative weight. A crescendo sentence builds momentum like rolling drums. Monotonous five-word chains drone and bore the ear.

---

## 4. Phonosemantics: Consonant Soundscapes

* **Plosives (/k/, /p/, /t/, /b/, /d/, /g/):** Staccato, violent (*"The cold cracked his knuckle bones"*).
* **Sibilants & Fricatives (/s/, /sh/, /f/, /v/, /th/):** Sinister, stealthy (*"The frost shifted softly across stone"*).
* **Liquids & Nasals (/l/, /r/, /m/, /n/):** Melancholic, lyrical (*"The river ran slow and dark beneath summer moon"*).

---

## 5. Asyndeton vs. Polysyndeton

* **Asyndeton (No Conjunctions):** *Item, item, item.* Accelerates chaos (*"He grabbed the bag, keys, gun, ran."*).
* **Polysyndeton (Many Conjunctions):** *Item and item and item.* Slows time with biblical weight (*"They carried the packs and the rifles and the dead child and the guilt."*).
