# Exposition Control & Conversational Masking

The crudest dialogue failure is **Maid-and-Butler Exposition** (*"As you know, Bob, our kingdom has been at war for ten years"*). Characters explain facts they both already know purely to brief the reader. Authentic speech operates through shared shorthand, tactical omission, and weaponized disclosure.

---

## 1. The Exposition Masking Hierarchy

Ranked from weakest to strongest:

```
[Level 1: Transparent Briefing] (Worst: Two allies calmly stating common lore)
            │
[Level 2: The Inquisitive Novice] (Passable: A recruit asks a necessary question)
            │
[Level 3: Disputed Interpretation] (Strong: Characters agree on fact, fight over meaning)
            │
[Level 4: Weaponized Exposition]   (Mastery: Lore delivered as an attack or leverage)
```

### 1. Disputed Interpretation
Characters never rehearse undisputed baseline facts. Instead, they clash over the consequences:
* *Weak:* *"As you know, the treaty expires at midnight."*
* *Strong:* *"The border captains won't wait for midnight. They've already fueled the transports."*

### 2. Weaponized Exposition
Information is introduced solely because speaking it inflicts emotional or political damage:
* *Example:* *"Remind me how many votes your father bought to secure this commission?"*
* *Result:* The reader learns the father bought the commission, but the primary drama is an act of aggression.

---

## 2. Techniques of Conversational Masking

1. **The Elliptical Shorthand:** Long-time associates use codes, nicknames, and fragments (*"The 09 protocol again?"* instead of reciting the protocol's history).
2. **The Pope in the Pool (Physical Staging):** When necessary technical lore must be delivered, bind the delivery to an intense physical distraction (treating a hemorrhage, defusing a fuse, docking a damaged ship).
3. **The Interrupted Disclosure:** Cut the speaker off with action or sharp resistance before they deliver a complete explanatory paragraph.

---

## 3. Diagnostic Audit Checklist

* [ ] **Shared Knowledge Audit:** Do both characters already know this information? If yes, why are they saying it out loud right now?
* [ ] **Hostile Utility:** Does delivering this information cost the speaker or hurt the listener?
* [ ] **Speech Chunk Sizing:** Are explanatory speeches broken after $\le 2$ sentences by physical resistance, skepticism, or deflection?

---

## 4. Multi-Path Revision Options

* **Option A (Submerge into Action):** Delete the spoken explanation entirely; reveal the lore through physical consequence.
* **Option B (Weaponize):** Turn the explanation into an accusation, taunt, or blackmailed demand.
* **Option C (Split Knowledge):** Give one character incorrect information so the correction creates interpersonal friction.
