# The Hitchcock Suspense Engine

Alfred Hitchcock famously distinguished between **Surprise** and **Suspense** using his "Bomb Under the Table" principle. In long-form fiction, suspense creates sustainable reader investment that surprise cannot match.

---

## 1. The Core Principle: Suspense vs. Surprise

*   **Surprise (15 Seconds of Shock):**
    *   Two characters sit having a mundane conversation. Suddenly, a bomb explodes. The reader experiences 15 seconds of surprise followed by cognitive reorientation.
*   **Suspense (15 Minutes of Agony):**
    *   The reader is shown a bomb placed under the table with the clock set for 1:00 PM. The characters sit down to have the exact same mundane conversation. The clock on the wall reads 12:45.
    *   *Effect:* Every mundane exchange ("Pass the salt," "How is your mother?") becomes excruciatingly tense. The reader actively yearns to intervene.

---

## 2. The Mechanics of the Ticking Clock

1. **Measurable Countdown:**
   * Abstract danger causes dread; a measurable limit creates panic. The clock can be literal (a train departure, dawn breaking, air supply percentage) or social (a wedding starting, an audit deadline).
2. **Compressing the Window:**
   * Escalate stakes by shortening the available time (a scheduled 2-hour delay evaporates into 20 minutes).
3. **The Micro-Obstacle Delay:**
   * Force characters to deal with trivial, frustrating roadblocks (a dropped key, a talkative neighbor, an elevator stall) while the reader knows the clock is expiring.

## 3. Application in SAGA Scene Beats
* In `scene_beat.md`, explicitly declare:
  - **What the reader knows:** (e.g., The killer is in the attic).
  - **What the POV character knows:** (e.g., Believes they are alone making tea).
  - **The ticking clock:** (e.g., Kettle boils in 2 minutes; floorboards creak).
