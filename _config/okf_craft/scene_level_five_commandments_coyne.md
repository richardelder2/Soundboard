# The Five Commandments of the Micro-Scene

In narrative craft, the scene is the **fundamental quantum of storytelling**. A novel is not a single 80,000-word monolith; it is a succession of 40 to 60 interconnected micro-scenes, each executing an autonomous dramatic arc.

Shawn Coyne’s Story Grid establishes that **every functional scene must execute five mandatory structural commandments**. If a scene lacks any of these five elements—most commonly the **Crisis Question**—it produces dead weight on the page, regardless of how poetic the prose may be.

---

## 1. The Five Atomic Commandments

```
[1. Inciting Incident]  --->  [2. Progressive Complications]
                                           │
                                           ▼
                                   [3. Crisis Question] (Binary Dilemma)
                                           │
                                           ▼
[5. Resolution & Shift]  <---  [4. Climax Decision]
```

### 1. The Inciting Incident (The Micro-Disruption)
*   **Function:** An event that upsets the local equilibrium at the start of the scene, giving the point-of-view character an immediate, urgent goal.
*   **Types:**
    *   *Causal:* An intentional act by another agent (e.g., an arrest warrant is served; an ally refuses an order; a rival demands a bribe).
    *   *Coincidental:* An unexpected environmental shift or accident (e.g., the engine dies; a blizzard traps the caravan; a letter arrives addressed to the wrong person).

### 2. Progressive Complications (Escalating Resistance)
*   **Function:** Obstacles and active pushback thrown up by the physical environment, social systems, or opposing characters that thwart the character’s initial, easy plan.
*   **The Law of Escalation:** Each successive obstacle must force the character to spend more energy, reveal more vulnerability, or raise their personal stakes.

### 3. The Crisis Question (The Core Engine)
*   **Function:** The heart of the scene. The character's easy options have evaporated. They are forced into a corner and must make an immediate, agonizing choice.
*   **The Two Pure Crisis Forms (Must Be a Strict Binary):**
    *   *Form A: Best Bad Choice:* Both available paths result in severe loss, pain, or sacrifice. Which cost is the character willing to pay? (e.g., reveal their secret identity to save an innocent, OR preserve their cover and watch the innocent hang).
    *   *Form B: Irreconcilable Goods:* Two deeply desirable, righteous outcomes cannot co-exist; choosing one permanently destroys the other (e.g., secure the gold that will free their enslaved family, OR use the escape route to save their wounded lover).

### 4. The Climax (The Irrevocable Action)
*   **Function:** The character executes their decision in active physical movement or spoken dialogue.
*   **Craft Invariant:** The climax cannot be an internal thought or passive acceptance; it must be an **externalized action that alters reality** and cannot be retracted.

### 5. The Resolution (The New Equilibrium & Value Shift)
*   **Function:** The immediate aftermath of the climactic decision.
*   **The Micro-Value Shift:** The scene must end with a tangible shift in the life-value state of the character or environment (e.g., *Trust $\rightarrow$ Suspicion*; *Safety $\rightarrow$ Mortal Peril*; *Ignorance $\rightarrow$ Horrifying Truth*).

---

## 2. The Scene Audit Checklist

Before approving any chapter draft, run the Five Commandments audit:
1.  **What is the Inciting Incident?** (Did something actually disrupt the status quo in the first 200 words?)
2.  **What were the progressive complications?** (Did the character face at least two escalating obstacles, or did they get what they wanted on the first try?)
3.  **What is the exact Crisis Question?** (Is it phrased as: *"Does Character choose X at the cost of A, or Y at the cost of B?"*)
4.  **What did the character physically do or say for the Climax?** (Did they cross an irreversible line?)
5.  **What was the value shift?** (Name the positive-to-negative or negative-to-positive transition).
