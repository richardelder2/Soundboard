# Story Grid Macro Architecture

The Story Grid methodology treats narrative as an engineering discipline. Every story—from a 1,500-word scene to a 120,000-word epic—must register a measurable **human value shift** (from positive to negative or vice versa) across three macro movements:

```
[ACT I: Beginning Hook]          [ACT II: Middle Build]          [ACT III: Ending Payoff]
      (0% - 25%)                       (25% - 75%)                     (75% - 100%)
Shifts Initial Value             Progressively Escalates Stakes   Delivers Global Value Climax
```

---

## 1. Global Story Value Spectrums

Every commercial genre is defined by a primary, non-negotiable **Global Human Value Spectrum**:

| Primary Genre | Positive State (+) | Negative State (-) | The Limit of the Human Condition (--) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Action / Thriller / Horror** | Life / Freedom | Threat / Injury | Death $ightarrow$ **Damnation / Fate Worse Than Death** |
| **Romance / Love Story** | Intimacy / Love | Misunderstanding / Friction | Hate $ightarrow$ **Self-Hatred / Total Isolation** |
| **Crime / Mystery** | Justice / Order | Injustice / Chaos | Tyranny $ightarrow$ **Institutional Corruption** |
| **Morality Arc** | Altruism / Integrity | Selfishness / Compromise | Betrayal $ightarrow$ **Pure Evil / Soul Corruption** |
| **Worldview / Maturation** | Wisdom / Sophistication | Naivety / Ignorance | Disillusionment $ightarrow$ **Meaninglessness / Cynicism** |

## 2. The 15 Core Macro Scenes (The 5 Commandments per Act)

In Story Grid, each of the three acts must execute all **Five Commandments of Storytelling**:

### Act I: The Beginning Hook (0% – 25%)
1. **Inciting Incident (BH):** Disrupts the protagonist's initial baseline equilibrium.
2. **Progressive Complication (BH):** Obstacles mount until the old life becomes unviable.
3. **Crisis (BH):** Dilemma forcing the hero to either stay in stasis or leap into the unknown.
4. **Climax (BH):** The irreversible decision to cross the threshold into the arena.
5. **Resolution (BH):** The new state of affairs establishing the Act II stakes.

### Act II: The Middle Build (25% – 75%)
6. **Inciting Incident (MB):** The first major confrontation in the new world.
7. **Progressive Complications (MB):** Multiple subplots, rivalries, and environmental pressures escalate toward the Midpoint.
8. **Crisis (MB - Midpoint / All Is Lost):** The devastating choice between irreconcilable goods or the lesser of two evils.
9. **Climax (MB):** The catastrophic choice that precipitates the All Is Lost moment.
10. **Resolution (MB):** Rock-bottom emotional status quo entering Act III.

### Act III: The Ending Payoff (75% – 100%)
11. **Inciting Incident (EP):** A final discovery, revelation, or enemy assault ignites the ultimate race.
12. **Progressive Complication (EP):** The opponent counters every move, tightening the noose.
13. **Crisis (EP - Core Event):** The ultimate existential dilemma testing the protagonist's transformed soul.
14. **Climax (EP):** The decisive action that resolves the global value for the entire manuscript.
15. **Resolution (EP):** The final image establishing the permanent new world order.

## 3. The Foolscap Global Story Plan

The Foolscap is the single-page constitutional document of the novel:
- **Global Genre:** Primary content genre + secondary commercial form.
- **Global Value Shift:** Start state to end state (e.g. *Naive Youth $ightarrow$ Hardened Leader*).
- **Controlling Idea / Theme:** Cause-and-effect sentence linking climax to meaning.
- **Obligatory Scenes Checklist:** The non-negotiable genre covenant scenes required by readers.
