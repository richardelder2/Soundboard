# The Short Story Engine: Single Effect, Compression, and Late Entry

A short story is not an abbreviated novel, nor is it a chapter without context. It operates under entirely distinct narrative physics. While a novel builds an expansive world of intersecting arcs, a short story is an arrow shot straight at a single psychological or emotional bullseye.

---

## 1. Poe's Unity of Effect (The Single Emotional Impression)

In a masterwork short story (1,000–7,500 words), every sentence, image, and cadence is chosen exclusively to generate one predetermined emotional impact—whether terror, yearning, dread, bitter irony, or sudden clarity.
*   **The Litmus Test:** If any paragraph, character quirk, or atmospheric flourish can be removed without diluting that solitary emotional payoff, it must be excised.

---

## 2. Core Operational Mechanics

### Late Point of Attack (In Medias Res)
*   Begin at the latest conceivable moment before the catastrophic or revelatory turn.
*   Do not explain how the characters arrived; drop the reader directly onto the thin ice of the unfolding situation.

### Extreme Narrative Economy
*   **Cast:** 1 to 3 characters maximum. Avoid subplots entirely.
*   **Setting:** A single compressed locus (one room, one evening, one journey).
*   **Backstory:** Delivered solely through immediate friction, never through expositional paragraphs.

### The Turn & The Epiphanic Payoff
*   Unlike novel climaxes which frequently feature physical triumph or external resolution, the short story typically hinges on an **epiphany**—a profound, irreversible shift in perception that leaves the world transformed for the protagonist, even if external circumstances remain unchanged.
