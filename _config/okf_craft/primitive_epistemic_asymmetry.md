# The Epistemic Gap: The Three Primal Narrative Drives

Every story—regardless of genre, medium, or culture—propels human attention forward through an **Epistemic Gap**: the differential between what the **Reader Knows**, what the **Protagonist Knows**, and what the **Antagonist/World Knows**.

Cognitive narratology (Meir Sternberg, David Bordwell) proves there are only **three fundamental epistemic engines**:

---

## 1. The Three Primal Engines

| Drive | Knowledge Equation | Human Cognitive Response | Primal Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mystery (Curiosity)** | `Protagonist Knows > Reader Knows` *(or both ignorant of the past)* | Mental reconstruction of past causes; decoding puzzle pieces. | *"What happened?"* |
| **Suspense (Anticipation)** | `Reader Knows > Protagonist Knows` *(Dramatic Irony)* | Visceral dread, breath constriction, yearning to warn/intervene. | *"What will happen when they find out?"* |
| **Dramatic Tension (Equal Unknowing)** | `Reader Knows == Protagonist Knows` *(Both facing an uncertain future)* | Empathic alignment, shared adrenaline, moral identification. | *"How will they survive this?"* |

---

## 2. Invariant Law of Narrative Energy

1. **Information Asymmetry Creates Potential Energy:** If everyone in a scene possesses the exact same information, dramatic voltage drops to zero.
2. **The Dynamic Triad:** A masterful story never relies on only one drive. It fluidly rotates:
   * Opens on a **Mystery** (a severed finger in the grass).
   * Shifts to **Dramatic Tension** (investigating the abandoned house in real time).
   * Elevates into **Suspense** (the reader sees the killer lurking in the closet while the detective steps inside).
