# Audience Calibration: Age Categories & Reader Horizons

Audience categories (Middle Grade, Young Adult, Adult, New Adult) are not censorship classifications; they are **developmental horizons and reader expectation frameworks**. Mismatching protagonist agency or thematic scope against target category limits market viability.

---

## 1. The Category Horizon Matrix

| Category | Typical Protagonist Age | Core Developmental Question | Agency & Authority | Pacing & Hope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Middle Grade (MG)** | 8–12 | *"How do I fit into my family and friendships?"* | Operates under adult rules; triumphs through peer solidarity and cleverness. | Fast-paced; fundamental hope preserved; world is fundamentally healable. |
| **Young Adult (YA)** | 16–18 | *"Who am I outside of my family, and what is my place in society?"* | Clashes directly with corrupt adult authority; assumes adult responsibility. | High immediacy, intense first-person/close-third, emotional urgency. |
| **New Adult (NA)** | 19–25 | *"How do I navigate independence, career, identity, and intimacy?"* | Total independence; navigating sexuality, professional entry, debt. | Visceral, relationship-forward, identity-defining crossroads. |
| **Adult Fiction** | Any age | *"How do I live with the consequences of past choices?"* | Full systemic agency; navigating legacy, compromise, disillusionment, grief. | Varied pacing; embraces permanent compromise and tragic ambiguity. |

---

## 2. Key Operational Calibration Rules

1. **Protagonist Age Anchor:** Protagonists should generally match or be 1–2 years older than the target audience (except in Adult fiction featuring child narrators like *To Kill a Mockingbird*).
2. **The Agency Test:** In MG and YA, adult mentors must not rescue the protagonist at the climax. The youth must make the climactic choice and bear the cost.
3. **Crossover Architecture:** Books that cross from YA to Adult (*The Hunger Games*, *Six of Crows*) combine sharp developmental agency with sophisticated systemic worldbuilding and unflinching moral consequences.

---

## 3. Multi-Path Revision Options

* **Option A (Recalibrate Agency):** If a YA protagonist is repeatedly saved by parents or mentors, remove the adults from the scene.
* **Option B (Adjust Thematic Lens):** If an MG story feels overly cynical, restore a note of communal solidarity and resilience.
* **Option C (Reflect Category Conventions):** Adjust vocabulary and interiority to reflect target developmental depth without dumbing down prose.
