# Brandon Sanderson's Three Laws of Magic & Magic System Engineering

In speculative fiction (fantasy, science fiction, and magical realism), magic and advanced speculative technology are not mere decorative spectacle. They are **dramatic problem-solving mechanisms** that operate under strict contracts with reader trust.

Brandon Sanderson codified three fundamental laws (plus a foundational Zeroth Law) that govern how speculative systems function within plot architecture without collapsing dramatic tension into cheap *deus ex machina*.

---

## 1. The Zeroth Law: The Primacy of Awesomeness & Narrative Compulsion

> *"Always err on the side of what is awesome."*

Before applying mathematical constraints, the magic or speculative concept must ignite genuine wonder, visceral drama, or aesthetic delight. If a system is mathematically rigorous but emotionally sterile, it fails as fiction. However, once the concept is established, it must immediately be bound by the Three Laws to preserve dramatic integrity.

---

## 2. Sanderson's First Law: The Problem-Solving Law

> *"An author’s ability to solve problems with magic in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic."*

This law establishes the continuum between **Hard Magic** and **Soft Magic**:

```
[SOFT MAGIC: Mystery & Awe] <==============================> [HARD MAGIC: Logic & Agency]
- Gandalf, Lovecraft, Miyazaki                               - Allomancy (Mistborn), Sympathy (Rothfuss)
- Reader does not know rules or limits.                      - Reader understands rules, costs, and limits.
- Used to CREATE problems and sense of wonder.               - Used to SOLVE problems through character agency.
- Must NEVER solve the climax (feels like cheating).         - Satisfies climax through clever exploitation of rules.
```

### The Climax Covenant:
- If a character escapes an inescapable trap using magic the reader did not know existed, the author cheated.
- If a character escapes using an established rule in a brilliantly unexpected combination, the reader experiences cathartic delight.

---

## 3. Sanderson's Second Law: The Law of Limitations

> *"Limitations are greater than powers."*

What a magic user **cannot do** or the **price they must pay** creates infinitely more dramatic tension than what they can do. Superman is compelling not because he can lift locomotives, but because of Kryptonite, his moral vow not to kill, and his inability to be in two places at once.

### The Five Dimensional Constraints of Magic:
1. **The Fuel / Ingestion Cost:** What scarce resource is consumed? (e.g., metals, blood, lifespans, battery charge, emotional sanity).
2. **The Hard Boundary (Absolute Negatives):** What can the magic *never* do? (e.g., cannot raise the dead, cannot create true gold, cannot manipulate human minds).
3. **The Backlash / Somatic Cost:** What physical, psychological, or environmental degradation occurs with use? (e.g., burns, exhaustion, attracting demonic predators, radiation).
4. **The Vulnerability Window:** Under what conditions is the user completely neutralized? (e.g., dampeners, sleep, running water, severed contact with the earth).
5. **The Sensory Signature:** What alerting phenomenon accompanies activation? (e.g., flashes, smell of ozone, acoustic humming that alerts city guards).

---

## 4. Sanderson's Third Law: The Extrapolation Law

> *"Expand what you already have before you add something new."*

Amateur worldbuilding solves narrative stagnation by introducing a second, third, or fourth magic system. Expert worldbuilding takes **one or two core magical premises** and extrapolates their second- and third-order consequences across every layer of civilization:

### The Extrapolation Cascade:
- **Military & Warfare:** If levitation exists, castles do not have tall vertical stone walls; they build recessed subterranean bunkers and overhead anti-drop nets.
- **Law & Crime:** If mind-reading or truth-telling spells exist, courtroom jurisprudence changes entirely; assassins develop cognitive decoy ciphers or poison themselves to protect employers.
- **Economy & Class:** Who controls the magic's fuel source? Does the aristocracy hoard the resource? Does a peasant who discovers an illegal vein disrupt the global currency?
- **Everyday Domestic Life:** How does the average citizen who possesses a minor spark of magic light their hearth, preserve food, or communicate with distant relatives?

---

## 5. The Magic System Design Matrix (Agent Diagnostic)

When planning a magic or speculative tech system in Stage 01/02, verify these 5 invariant parameters:

| Parameter | Diagnostic Question | Red Flag (AI Tell) |
|---|---|---|
| **Source & Fuel** | What fuels the power and where is it obtained? | Infinite, free power without scarcity. |
| **Clear Costs** | What does the user lose with each exertion? | Power used freely without physical or moral consequence. |
| **Strict Limitations** | What are the 3 non-negotiable things this power cannot do? | Magic can accomplish whatever the scene requires. |
| **Societal Footprint** | How has society evolved around this power's existence? | Medieval Europe clone where magic somehow changed nothing. |
| **Climactic Payoff** | Is the final victory won by a clever combination of established rules? | Sudden new spell discovered in the final chapter. |
