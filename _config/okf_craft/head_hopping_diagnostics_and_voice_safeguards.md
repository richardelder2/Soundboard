# Head-Hopping Diagnostics & Voice Safeguards

Accidental **head-hopping** occurs when third-person limited narration slips unannounced into another character's cognitive stream. However, agents must not mistake deliberate omniscient narration, polyphonic counterpoint, or free indirect style for an error.

---

## 1. Diagnostic Triage

* **Accidental Head-Hop:** Close third anchored in Character A suddenly reports Character B's private sensations (*"Elena shivered at the cold. Marcus wondered why she was afraid."*). $\rightarrow$ **Flag for revision.**
* **Deliberate Omniscience:** A distinct narrator voice holds panoramic authority over the scene, evaluating multiple minds with consistent tonal distance. $\rightarrow$ **Preserve.**
* **Stylistic Fragmentation:** Impressionistic prose blurring boundaries deliberately as a thematic device. $\rightarrow$ **Preserve.**

---

## 2. The "Do Not Flatten Voice" Safeguard

AI editors frequently over-correct stylistic choices into generic prose. **Do not modify:**
1. **Reported Inference:** *"He looked terrified"* is external deduction, not head-hopping.
2. **Free Indirect Free-play:** When narration blends with community gossip or collective mood.
3. **Intentional Distance:** Cool detachment or fairy-tale telling with minimal interiority.

---

## 3. Four-Path Revision Playbook

When an accidental head-hop is diagnosed, offer the writer:

* **Option A (Anchor Single POV):** Re-anchor the beat in the primary focalizer. Turn the second character's thoughts into physical action.
* **Option B (Section Break):** Insert a scene divider (`***`) and establish the new focalizer clearly in the opening sentence.
* **Option C (Cinematic Exterior):** Step back to Level 2 (Objective Exterior), letting physical blocking carry subtext.
* **Option D (Preserve as Intent):** Confirm whether the author intended classic omniscience.
