# Viewpoint-Filtered Sensory Selection

A scene should never present a detached, exhaustive inventory of room furnishings. The human nervous system is an aggressive editing filter: we perceive only what our immediate hunger, professional training, acute terror, or ingrained prejudices force us to notice. **All description is characterization.**

---

## 1. The Perceptual Bias Engine

```
[Raw Physical Space] ──> [Filter: Character Profession + Acute Emotion] ──> [Selected 20% on Page]
```

* **The Carpenter entering a cathedral:** Notices cedar trusses, dovetail joinery, green wood checking, load-bearing cracks.
* **The Pickpocket entering the same space:** Notices sightlines from the vestibule, coin purses tucked into belts, echoing flagstones, velvet drape shadows.
* **The Grieving Mother entering the same space:** Notices the cold draft across the pews, child-sized kneelers, flickering wax smell reminding her of a vigil.

---

## 2. Camera-Eye vs. Visceral Perception

| Camera-Eye Inventory (Flawed) | Viewpoint-Filtered Selection (Mastery) |
|---|---|
| *"The tavern was 30 feet wide with six oak tables, three lanterns, and a fireplace where mutton roasted."* | *"The tavern was suffocatingly warm, smelling of scorched mutton grease and damp wool coats that had hung too close to the hearth."* |
| *"He had brown hair, blue eyes, a scar on his chin, and wore a leather vest over a linen tunic."* | *"He had the squint of an archer and a jagged white scar dragging the corner of his mouth into a permanent sneer."* |

---

## 3. Protecting Restrained and Sparse Prose

AI editorial audits often mistakenly demand more adjectives and sensory layers in every paragraph. **Do not over-describe:**
* In fast-paced action or clinical horror, sparse, austere description heightens visceral tension.
* Honor the **Sensory Budget**: 1–2 sharp, unexpected sensory details beat an exhausting inventory of sight, sound, smell, touch, and taste.

---

## 4. Multi-Path Revision Options

* **Option A (The Professional Filter):** Recast description using vocabulary native to the character's trade or trauma.
* **Option B (The Sensory Prune):** Cut three visual descriptions; replace them with one tactile or thermal sensation.
* **Option C (Protect Sparse Voice):** If the writer intentionally uses austere Hemingway-esque or hardboiled restraint, confirm and preserve.
