# Walter Murch's Rule of Six Applied to Prose Pacing

When cutting between paragraphs, scenes, or chapters, apply Walter Murch's hierarchy of editing criteria to ensure prose maintains emotional momentum rather than mere mechanical continuity.

---

## The Hierarchy of Narrative Cuts

1. **Emotion (51% Priority):**
   * Does the sentence break, paragraph transition, or scene cut honor the emotional truth of the moment? If a detail is technically accurate but deflates emotional tension, cut it immediately.
2. **Story / Plot Forward Motion (23% Priority):**
   * Does this beat advance the central dramatic question or shift a character value? Strip away transitional travelogue ("He walked to the car, drove down the highway...") and jump straight to the point of change.
3. **Rhythm / Cadence (10% Priority):**
   * Sentence length variance (CV) mirrors cutting speed. Short staccato sentences mimic rapid action cuts; expansive, rolling clauses create slow, reflective pans.
4. **Eye Trace / Reader Focus (7% Priority):**
   * Guide the reader's mental lens fluidly. The focal point at the end of paragraph A (e.g., a hand reaching into a pocket) should logically bridge to the opening focus of paragraph B (the cold grip of steel).
5. **Spatial Geometry & 2D Mental Map (5% Priority):**
   * Maintain the reader's intuitive mental orientation of where people stand relative to exits, windows, and each other.
6. **Physical Action Logic (4% Priority):**
   * Realistic mechanics come *last*. If emotion, story, and rhythm are firing on all cylinders, minor physical pedantry is irrelevant to reader engagement.
