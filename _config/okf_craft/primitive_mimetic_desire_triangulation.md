# Mimetic Desire: The Mechanics of Human Jealousy & Rivalry

Human beings do not desire objects or people spontaneously. Human desire is **Mimetic (Imitative)**: we desire things because **someone else desires them**.

René Girard's foundational anthropology proves that all narrative conflict is driven by **Triangular Desire**:

```
           [MEDIATOR / MODEL / RIVAL]
                  ▲         ▲
                 /           \
        (Desires)             (Imitates Desire)
               /               \
              ▼                 ▼
          [OBJECT] <─────── [SUBJECT / PROTAGONIST]
```

---

## 1. The Three Nodes of Mimetic Conflict

1. **The Subject (Protagonist):** Believes their passion, ambition, or attraction is original and authentic.
2. **The Object:** The trophy, promotion, romantic partner, throne, or holy relic. The object itself is inherently neutral; its value is purely an artifact of social projection.
3. **The Mediator (The Model / The Rival):** The person whose gaze or possession confers value upon the object.

---

## 2. Invariant Laws of Mimetic Escalation

*   **The Rivalry Trap:** As the Subject and the Mediator fight harder for the Object, the Object itself becomes irrelevant. The conflict transforms into a deadly obsession with the **Rival themselves** (e.g., *The Prestige*, *Amadeus*, *Gatsby*).
*   **The Scapegoat Mechanism (Crisis Resolution):** When mimetic envy threatens to tear a community or cast apart, the collective resolves their internal chaos by selecting a single, vulnerable **Scapegoat** upon whom all collective guilt is projected and expelled.
