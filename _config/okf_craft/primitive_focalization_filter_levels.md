# Focalization: The Primitive Architecture of Perception

Point of view is frequently confused with pronouns ("first person" vs. "third person"). But cognitive narratology demonstrates that the primitive engine of narrative perception is **Focalization**: *Who sees vs. Who speaks*.

---

## 1. The Three Primitive States of Focalization

1. **Zero Focalization (Omniscient God's-Eye):**
   * *Formula:* `Narrator Knows > Character Knows`.
   * *Perception:* The narrator can read all minds simultaneously, comment on historical outcomes centuries in the future, and perceive secret rooms.
   * *Effect:* Panoramic historical scale (Tolstoy, Victor Hugo). Risk: emotional detachment.
2. **Internal Focalization (The Character's Mind Filter):**
   * *Formula:* `Narrator Knows == Character Knows`.
   * *Fixed:* The lens is locked to a single character for the entire book/chapter.
   * *Variable:* The lens moves between Character A and Character B in alternating chapters (*Game of Thrones*).
   * *Multiple:* The same event is experienced through different eyes (*The Sound and the Fury*).
3. **External Focalization (The Unreadable Camera):**
   * *Formula:* `Narrator Knows < Character Knows`.
   * *Perception:* The camera records physical gestures, dialogue, and clothing, but **never** enters the character's thoughts or feelings.
   * *Effect:* Intense suspense, cold procedural dread, enigmatic behavior (Dashiell Hammett's *The Maltese Falcon*, Hemingway's *The Killers*).
