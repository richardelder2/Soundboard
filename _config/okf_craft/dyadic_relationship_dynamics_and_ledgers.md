# Dyadic Relationship Dynamics & Relational Ledgers

Characters do not exist in isolation; they exist in **dyadic relational ecosystems**. When relationships stagnate, characters become static sounding boards. Every meaningful relationship must be tracked across multiple dynamic currents that fluctuate across chapters.

---

## 1. The Seven Relational Vectors

For any primary character pair (Protagonist/Foil, Protagonist/Mentor, Rivalry, Romance), monitor seven key vectors:

```
[Trust (0-10)]        : Belief in the other's integrity under pressure.
[Status Differential] : Who yields the floor, who commands, who defers.
[Debt / Obligation]   : Unsettled moral, financial, or mortal favors.
[Desire / Attachment] : Longing for approval, physical intimacy, or connection.
[Fear / Vulnerability]: Capacity of the other to inflict mortal or emotional harm.
[Resentment]          : Unexpressed grievances and accumulated slights.
[Information Balance] : Who knows what about whom (secrets, leverage, blackmail).
```

---

## 2. The Dyadic Relationship Ledger Matrix

| Pair | Initial Polarity (Act I) | Key Midpoint Pivot | Climactic Collision (Act III) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero & Mentor** | Reverence; total deference | Discovery of mentor's past compromise | Hero surpasses mentor or rejects advice |
| **Hero & Rival** | Mutual contempt; status war | Forced alliance against common threat | Mutual grudging respect or fatal betrayal |
| **Hero & Love Interest**| Defensive friction; hidden wound| Vulnerable sanctuary breached | Sacrificing external want for mutual truth |

---

## 3. Diagnosing Stagnant Relationships

1. **The Static Ally Syndrome:** An ally who exists only to validate the protagonist, provide transportation, or nod sympathetically. **Fix:** Give the ally a competing private ambition that clashes directly with the protagonist's timeline.
2. **Unearned Emotional Leaps:** Characters shifting from mortal hatred to fierce devotion without dramatized concessions or shared trauma. **Fix:** Insert an intermediate step of grudging tactical truce.
3. **The Disappearing Debt:** A character owes a life debt or financial ransom, but it is forgotten once the action accelerates. **Fix:** Have the creditor demand payment at the worst possible moment.

---

## 4. Multi-Path Revision Options

* **Option A (Inject Status Shift):** Invert the status dynamic during an routine exchange (the subordinate holds crucial information; the master must plead).
* **Option B (Call the Debt):** Force a secondary character to withhold assistance until a past emotional or material debt is settled.
* **Option C (Erupt Resentment):** Allow an accumulated minor grievance to boil over during an unrelated high-stakes crisis.
