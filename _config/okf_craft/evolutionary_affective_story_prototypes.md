# Affective Narratology & Evolutionary Story Prototypes

Narrative structure is not an arbitrary cultural invention. In Patrick Colm Hogan's **Affective Narratology**, cross-cultural story structures across all human societies are governed by universal neuro-emotional systems rooted in human evolutionary biology.

Stories attain universal catharsis because they systematically engage the evolutionary imperatives required for human survival and social flourishing.

---

## 1. The Three Pan-Cultural Story Prototypes

Hogan identifies three universal story architectures found across every documented human culture:

### 1. The Heroic Prototype (In-Group Defense & Survival)
*   **Evolutionary Imperative:** Defense of the kin-group or tribe against external predators, rival tribes, and physical annihilation.
*   **The Narrative Arc:**
    1.  *Threat:* An external predator, monster, or hostile army disrupts the group's sanctuary.
    2.  *Trial:* The designated defender must undergo physical/strategic trials to prove combat competence and loyalty.
    3.  *Confrontation:* Direct, high-stakes battle against the existential threat.
    4.  *Resolution:* Securing the tribe’s perimeter, preserving communal resources, and elevating the hero’s social standing.

### 2. The Romantic Prototype (Pair-Bonding & Social Continuity)
*   **Evolutionary Imperative:** Sexual selection, overcoming reproductive barriers, and forming stable pair-bonds amidst clan divisions.
*   **The Narrative Arc:**
    1.  *Attraction:* Mutual recognition of genetic, spiritual, or emotional complementarity.
    2.  *The Social Divide:* Filial prohibitions, class hierarchies, ancient family vendettas, or rival suitors block union.
    3.  *Separation & Tribulation:* The lovers endure geographic distance, misunderstandings, or mortal trials.
    4.  *Resolution:* Overcoming the societal barrier to achieve mutual devotion, validated by communal recognition (the wedding or legitimate union).

### 3. The Sacrificial Prototype (Communal Crisis & Scapegoat Renewal)
*   **Evolutionary Imperative:** Resolving internal social decay, civil war, environmental famine, or disease epidemic that threatens the collective whole.
*   **The Narrative Arc:**
    1.  *The Blight:* The society experiences moral corruption, civil strife, or ecological collapse that traditional laws cannot remedy.
    2.  *The Identification of Guilt:* The crisis is recognized as requiring a supreme personal or ritual restitution.
    3.  *The Selfless Transgression/Offering:* A noble agent willingly accepts exile, suffering, or death to purge the collective corruption.
    4.  *Resolution:* The society’s moral and ecological equilibrium is restored through the cathartic sacrifice of the individual.

## 2. Somatosensory Appraisal: The Cognitive Emotion Cycle

In evolutionary psychology, emotions are rapid somatic programs that prioritize goals before conscious deliberation occurs:

```
[Sensory Registration: Sudden visual/auditory shift]
                         │
                         ▼
[Affective Appraisal: Fast somatic evaluation - Does this threaten survival or love?]
                         │
                         ▼
[Actional Orientation: Nervous system primes body for fight, flight, or connection]
```

### Craft Rule for High-Resonance Prose:
Whenever a character experiences a plot twist, do not summarize their feelings abstractly (e.g., *"He felt terrified"*). Trace the evolutionary appraisal cycle:
1.  **Sensory Cue:** The cold metallic scrape of a latch, the drop in room temperature.
2.  **Somatic Spike:** Adrenaline dumping into the chest, constriction of the throat, hair rising on the forearms.
3.  **Action Impulse:** Body bracing against the wall, shifting weight to the balls of the feet, eyes locking on the nearest exit.
