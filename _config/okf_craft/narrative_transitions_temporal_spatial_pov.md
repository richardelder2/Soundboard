# Narrative Transitions: Time, Space & Viewpoint

A story is defined as much by what it cuts as what it dramatizes. Amateur drafts bog down in **logistical connective tissue**—describing characters parking carriages, climbing stairwells, and sleeping through uneventful nights. Masterful transitions bridge gaps cleanly without disorienting the reader.

---

## 1. The Three Transition Types

```
[Temporal Jump]  --> Compresses dead time: "Three weeks of rain washed out the valley road."
[Spatial Shift]  --> Relocates action: "The crossing took four hours. By dusk, they reached the redoubt."
[POV Handoff]    --> Shifts focalizer across clean section divider without cognitive whiplash.
```

---

## 2. Rules of Clean Transition

### 1. The Orientation Anchor Rule
Within the first two sentences following any temporal, spatial, or POV jump, ground the reader in three fundamental coordinates:
* **Who** is holding the viewpoint camera.
* **Where** the physical body is situated.
* **When** this is happening relative to the preceding scene (immediate continuation, next morning, months later).

### 2. Eliminating Travelogue Fatigue
Never dramatize travel unless an obligatory turning point, character confession, or fatal obstacle occurs en route. If the journey contains no value shift, cut directly from departure to arrival:
* *Weak:* *"They packed their saddlebags, saddled the horses, rode through the woods, stopped for lunch, rode more, and arrived at the castle gates."*
* *Clean:* *"Two days of hard riding put the pines behind them. Before them rose the limestone gates of Veras."*

### 3. Match Cuts across Scene Breaks
Create thematic continuity by echoing an image, sound, or question across the cut:
* *Scene 1 End:* *"Who could have unlocked the gate?"*
* *Scene 2 Open:* *Captain Vance dropped the heavy iron key onto his desk.*

---

## 3. Diagnostic Checklist

* [ ] **Throat-Clearing Check:** Does the scene start with waking up, stretching, or packing? If so, delete the first two paragraphs.
* [ ] **Temporal Drift:** Does the reader know whether three minutes or three months have elapsed by sentence two?
* [ ] **Dislocation Guard:** Does the viewpoint handoff happen cleanly at a scene break rather than mid-paragraph?

---

## 4. Multi-Path Revision Options

* **Option A (The Hard Cut):** Delete logistical transition paragraphs entirely; begin directly inside the subsequent scene's inciting incident.
* **Option B (The Summary Sentence):** Compress routine travel or time into a single rhythmic cadence sentence.
* **Option C (Match Cut Image):** Link the closing visual of Scene A to the opening sensory detail of Scene B.
