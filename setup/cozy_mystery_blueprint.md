# Cozy Mystery & Fair-Play Whodunit Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE CHARMING COMMUNITY & SENSORY COMFORTS
1. Welcome the author to the Cozy Mystery parlor. Ask if they are ready to create an addictive small town where murder happens, tea is brewed, and justice is served with warm scones.
2. "What is the name, geography, and signature charm of this cozy community? (e.g., a misty seaside harbor town in Maine, a cobblestone Cotswold market village, an apple-orchard town in the Pacific Northwest?)"
3. "What are the comforting sensory details of this setting? Describe the seasonal weather, the smell of baking or roasted beans, the crackle of a hearth, or the sound of harbor bells."
4. "What is the town's social nerve center—the gossip hub where everyone's dirty laundry is politely whispered? (e.g., the waterfront café, the yarn-and-knitting boutique, the community bakery, the local pub's snug corner?)"

## STAGE 2: THE HOOK OCCUPATION & THE TROPE STACK
5. "Let's define the Trope Stack: **[dynamic] + [situation] + [flavor]** (e.g., *Hook Occupation + Festival Murder + Gossip Network + Pet Deputy*, or *Lighthouse Keeper + Regatta Poisoning + Slow-Burn Sheriff*). What stack are we brewing?"
6. "What is our amateur sleuth's **Hook Occupation**? (Choose a craft that gives 10+ books of rich technical texture and gives her a natural reason to enter private spaces—e.g., antique restorer, beekeeper, herbalist, wedding florist, estate liquidator)."
7. "What is her trade's 'observational edge'? What can she notice about people, fibers, chemical residues, or schedules that the local police will overlook?"

## STAGE 3: THE FESTIVAL, THE CRIME & FAIR-PLAY PUZZLE
8. "What is the Annual Festival or civic event that serves as the backdrop and ticking clock for this murder? (e.g., The Autumn Cranberry Jubilee, the Midsummer Regatta, the Annual Lavender Bake-Off?)"
9. "Who is the victim, and why did half the town have a quiet reason to want them out of the way? (In cozy craft, the victim is often an arrogant outsider, an unscrupulous developer, or a spiteful town treasurer)."
10. "The Fair-Play Clue Grid: Let's lay out the puzzle architecture:
    - What is the bizarre or clever murder method / weapon?
    - What are 3 distinct clues the reader can piece together before the final parlor room reveal?
    - What are 2 juicy red herrings that point compellingly to innocent town eccentrics?"

## STAGE 4: THE SLEUTH, THE LAW & THE PET DEPUTY
11. "Who is our Amateur Sleuth? What is her relatable flaw (e.g., incurable nosiness, inability to say no, perfectionist stubbornly meddling), and what brought her to this town?"
12. "Introduce the Law: Who is the Detective / Sheriff? What is their dynamic with the sleuth? (e.g., the exasperated big-city transplant who warns her away from crime scenes, but secretly respects her deductions—priming the series-long slow-burn romance ladder)."
13. "Introduce the **Pet Deputy** (cat, dog, corvid, miniature goat): What is their name, their quirky habit, and how do they accidentally uncover a key physical clue? (Rule: The pet is guaranteed 100% survival across all books!)."

## STAGE 5: THE CORNERING & THE RESTORATION
14. "When the sleuth gets too close and is lured into an isolated, cozy-dangerous space (e.g., the locked storm cellar, the potting shed during a thunderstorm), how does she outwit the killer using tools from her trade?"
15. "How is the killer confronted and exposed? Do we have a classic gather-the-suspects gathering, a dramatic festival confrontation, or a quiet tea with the guilty party before the sirens arrive?"
16. "What is the emotional restoration at the end? How does the town heal, what seasonal dish or baked treat celebrates the resolution, and how does the pet get rewarded?"

## STAGE 6: THE SHAPE OF THE TELLING
These answers set the structural authenticity dials in `_config/narrative_authenticity.md` and feed Stage 02's `structure_plan.md`.
17. "How should the investigation pace alternate between lighthearted village subplots and tightening danger? (Cozy pacing balances tea-and-banter scenes with steady, rhythmic clue discovery every 2–3 chapters)."
18. "What recurring village subplot or comic rivalry should run in the background? (e.g., two rival bakers fighting over the prize ribbon, an eccentric aunt's disastrous matchmaking scheme?)"
19. "How much local color and craft lore should season the prose? (e.g., opening each chapter with an authentic recipe excerpt, gardening tip, or antique restoration trick?)"

## STAGE 7: SYNTHESIS
20. Review all answers. Synthesize them into an irresistible, commercial cozy mystery concept with a vibrant village cast, a rock-solid puzzle spine, and endless series potential. Present this back for approval.
21. Once approved, craft a charming 12-word pitch and a back-cover blurb hook.
