# Folk Horror & Gothic Dread Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE SEDUCTIVE COMMUNITY & PASTORAL CHILL
1. Welcome the author to the mist-shrouded valley of Folk Horror. Ask if they are ready to craft a story of creeping dread where sunlight is terrifying and community kindness conceals ancient hunger.
2. "Describe the isolated setting. (e.g., a sunlit Scandinavian island commune with wildflowers and timber halls, an ancient Cornish mining village surrounded by moorland barrows, an Appalachian hollow cut off by river fog)? Why is it rendered genuinely seductive and beautiful on page one?"
3. "What are the tactile sensory indicators of dread? Describe the scent of damp earth and woodsmoke, the rhythm of church bells slightly out of tune, the taste of clover honey with a metallic aftertaste, or symbols freshly painted on door lintels."
4. "The **Sincerity Law of Folk Horror**: The villagers are NOT sneering Hollywood cultists. They are gentle, loving, and deeply devout. How do they treat the outsider protagonist with warm, disarming hospitality that makes paranoia feel rude?"

## STAGE 2: THE TROPE STACK & THE RITE CLOCK
5. "Let's lock in your Trope Stack: **[dynamic] + [situation] + [flavor]**
   - Flagship Stack: *Folk Horror + Missing-Person Spine + Dual Timeline*
   - Gothic Variant: *Inherited Manor + Decaying Aristocracy + Body Horror*
   - Ecological Dread Variant: *Remote Research Outpost + Pagan Awakening + Fungal Isolation*
   What nightmare territory are we exploring?"
6. "What is the **Old Practice / Ancient Bargain**? What do the villagers appease, feed, or maintain, and what agrarian prosperity or protection does the entity provide in return?"
7. "What is the **Rite Calendar (The Ticking Clock)**? What ancient festival or solstice is the story bending toward? When the festival begins, what threshold cannot be uncrossed?"

## STAGE 3: THE MISSING PERSON & DUAL TIMELINE
8. "The **Missing Person (The Thriller Spine)**: Who vanished here prior to the story? What was their emotional connection to the outsider protagonist (a sister, an estranged child, a former lover, a fellow academic)?"
9. "What is the official village story regarding the missing person ('She packed her bags in the night and took the train'), and what was the horrifying truth?"
10. "The **Dual Timeline**: How do the two timelines mirror and rhyme with each other?
    - **NOW Timeline**: The protagonist's desperate investigation and polite gaslighting:
    - **THEN Timeline**: The missing person's gradual, joyful seduction by the community (the cruelest note: they were *happy* here):"

## STAGE 4: THE OUTSIDER & THE ANCIENT ENTITY
11. "Who is our Outsider Protagonist? What rational, scientific, or legal background do they rely on as mental armor, and what personal grief makes them vulnerable to the community's promises of belonging?"
12. "Who is the **Community Shepherd** (The village elder, priest, matriarch, or doctor)? Describe their calm, radiant certainty and utter absence of malice."
13. "What are the rules and nature of the **Entity / Ancient Force**?
    - What can it manifest or control?
    - What is its escalation logic?
    - What is its strict, letter-of-the-deal contract?"

## STAGE 5: THE SCREENSHOT IMAGE & THE UNFORGIVING ENDING
14. "The **Screenshot Image (~60%)**: In modern prestige horror, there is ONE unforgettable visual set-piece that readers and reviewers will talk about forever (e.g., the wall of identical smiling photographs in the barn, the hollow wicker effigy standing in a field of red poppies, the missing person sitting motionless at the head of a flower banquet). Describe this image with cinematic clarity."
15. "The **Polite Isolation Mechanics**: When the protagonist realizes the danger and tries to flee, how does the village gently, smilingly prevent their escape without ever raising a fist? ('The road is washed out,' 'The bus only runs after solstice,' 'We took your car keys so you wouldn't worry')."
16. "The **Ending Contract**: Horror may end dark, but it must land authored and earned. Choose your ending:
    - *Escape at Terrible Cost* (fleeing, but carrying the seed/debt back to the modern world):
    - *The Old Way Wins* (the outsider is consumed, chosen, or sacrificed for the harvest):
    - *Conversion* (the outsider willingly joins the dance and embraces the ancient pact):"
