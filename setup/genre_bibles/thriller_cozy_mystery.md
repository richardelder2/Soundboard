# SERIES BIBLE & TEMPLATE: Thriller / Mystery / Cozy Mystery
*Two full templates, because this cluster runs two different businesses: (A) the standalone **Domestic Thriller** under a branded pen name (stack: Perfect-Life Lie + Gaslight Engine + Techno-Paranoia — the 2026-fresh chassis), and (B) the long-running **Cozy Mystery series** (stack: Hook Occupation + Festival Murder + Gossip Network + Pet Deputy).*

---

# TEMPLATE A: DOMESTIC THRILLER (standalone, branded pen name)

## A1. BOOK BIBLE (fill-in — new per book; the PEN NAME is the series)

- **Pen-name promise:** "A [PEN NAME] book: you won't guess it, it plays fair, one sitting."
- **12-word pitch:** [e.g., "Her smart home's new update keeps locking the doors at 3 a.m."]
- **The enviable surface:** the life readers should covet for 3 chapters [house/marriage/job ___]
- **THE LIE (the book's engine):** what is actually true [___] · who architected it [___] · their meticulous motive [___] — write the villain's full timeline FIRST; the book is the reader discovering it out of order
- **The tech vector:** [smart home/AI assistant/tracking app/deepfake ___] · what convenience it sold [___] · how it becomes the cage [___] · the analog escape [___]
- **POV structure (choose):** [single unreliable / dual now-then / dual her-and-hidden-architect]
- **The twist (write it in one sentence):** [___] · **Twist-ledger check:** confirm it matches nothing in the last 2 quarters' top-50 ledger (Trope Master D5) [date checked ___]
- **The 3+ planted clues (fair-play law):** [clue 1, ch. ___] [clue 2, ch. ___] [clue 3, ch. ___] — a rereader must find all of them
- **Evidence-both-ways grid (gaslight engine):** for each strange event, one entry supporting "she's unraveling" and one supporting "someone's doing this" [table ___]

## A2. BEAT SHEET — chapter skeleton (~85k words, ~45 chapters of 1,800–2,200; short chapters ARE the genre)

**ACT 1 (ch. 1–11, 0–25%)**
- Ch. 1: **Flash-forward prologue** — blood/sirens/one wrong image, then "[X] weeks earlier." (The cliché that converts; the image must recontextualize on arrival.)
- Ch. 2–4: The enviable life, rendered genuinely enviable. Seed clue #1 invisibly inside the charm.
- Ch. 5–6: Wrongness #1 — small, deniable [the moved object / the setting she didn't change / the login at 3 a.m.]. She explains it away; the reader can't quite.
- Ch. 7–8: The tech's convenience showcased — and its access quietly inventoried (what it sees, whom it reports to).
- Ch. 9–10: Wrongness #2, undeniable to HER, deniable to everyone else. The first "am I losing it?" beat. Evidence-both-ways grid activates.
- Ch. 11: Act turn — she decides to investigate quietly. A confidant enlisted [flag: the untrustworthy-ally seed goes here].

**ACT 2A (ch. 12–23, 25–50%)**
- Ch. 12–14: Investigation vs. gaslight escalation in alternation — every answer she finds, the architect counters. Clue #2 planted inside a counter-move.
- Ch. 15–16: Social-performance set-piece [dinner party/school gala] — the perfect life performed while cracking; someone notices and is dismissed (they'll matter).
- Ch. 17–19: Her credibility attacked externally [the concerned call to her husband/doctor/boss]. Isolation tightens. The tech now actively curates her reality [filtered messages / edited footage].
- Ch. 20–22: She finds the first hard thread [a receipt, a prior owner, a name] — the past-life of the house/husband/app opens.
- Ch. 23: **MIDPOINT REVERSAL:** the proof-object scene — one undeniable thing (obligatory). But acquiring it exposes her; the architect now knows she knows.

**ACT 2B (ch. 24–34, 50–75%)**
- Ch. 24–26: The game turns cat-and-mouse; she plays sane while gathering. Clue #3 planted. The confidant's inconsistency #1.
- Ch. 27–29: Counter-strike: the architect's biggest move [she's locked out—of accounts, the house, her own history; a public incident stages her instability].
- Ch. 30–32: Lowest point: institutional doors close [police polite, doctor concerned, husband "worried"]. She recognizes the design: every escape was pre-blocked. (The reader should feel the architecture's elegance here — that's the product.)
- Ch. 33–34: The analog pivot — she goes offline/old-school [paper, a stranger's phone, the one unsurveilled place] and finds the architect's single overlooked thread.

**ACT 3 (ch. 35–45, 75–100%)**
- Ch. 36–38: **THE TWIST (85–90%):** the reveal that recontextualizes [the ally is the architect / the victim orchestrated it / her memory is the crime scene]. Immediately show 2 of the 3 planted clues in flashback-flash (reward the rereader in real time).
- Ch. 39–41: Confrontation on her terms — she uses the architect's own system against them [the tech that caged her testifies for her]. Physical jeopardy compressed and fast.
- Ch. 42–43: Resolution: the lie's full architecture laid out, no loose threads (the denouement readers grade you on). Justice lands.
- Ch. 44–45: The after — what she keeps, what she leaves; final line earns the prologue's image. Optional last-page sting IF honestly seeded (never a cheap "or is it?").

**Back matter:** "the clues you missed" bonus page (review-bait, delightful) → next-book preorder → newsletter.

# TEMPLATE B: COZY MYSTERY SERIES (15-book architecture)

## B1. SERIES BIBLE (fill-in)

- **Sleuth:** name [___] · **hook occupation** [pick for 15 books of material — unusual trades are open lanes: beekeeper/lighthouse keeper/festival organizer] · her edge (what her trade lets her NOTICE) [___] · her flaw [nosiness/stubbornness — endearing scale]
- **Town:** name [___] · the annual event calendar [4+ festivals = 4+ murder venues] · the gossip network's hub [salon/café/knitting circle ___] · comic-relief informant [___]
- **The law:** Detective/Sheriff [___] — skeptic→grudging-respect arc across books 1–3; the series-long slow-burn romance ladder (see trackers; NEVER resolves faster than one milestone per 2 books)
- **The pet deputy:** [cat/dog ___] · its clue-finding quirk [___] · survival guaranteed
- **Series-long threads:** the sleuth's business growth ledger [___] · the town-secrets ledger (one burned per book) [___] · the returning rival [health inspector/rival shop ___]
- **Title pattern:** [occupation pun + crime pun: "A Batch Made in Heaven," "Fatal Frosting" — write 15 titles NOW; if you can't, change occupations]

## B2. BEAT SHEET — chapter skeleton (~62k words, ~26 chapters of 2,400)

**ACT 1 (ch. 1–7)**
- Ch. 1: Sleuth in her element — occupation charm + the festival/event preparations. Every suspect walks through her shop (closed-circle law: all on stage before the body).
- Ch. 2: Frictions displayed: the victim-to-be is mildly unlikable to 4–5 people ON PAGE, each with a visible grievance (your suspect grid, planted as gossip).
- Ch. 3: **The body** — discovered (never witnessed), offscreen violence, at/near the event. The sleuth finds it (or her pet does).
- Ch. 4: The law arrives; the skeptic beat ("leave it to us, [occupation] lady"); a detail only her trade-eye catches [the wrong flour on the shoes] — dismissed.
- Ch. 5–6: The gossip network activates — intelligence gathering disguised as [haircuts/coffee/choir]. Suspect grid formalized: 5 suspects × motive × secret × alibi.
- Ch. 7: Personal stakes lock in [her friend/shop/festival is implicated]. She's in.

**ACT 2 (ch. 8–19) — the clue/red-herring alternator (strict rhythm: revelation every 2 chapters, alternating true clue / red herring)**
- Ch. 8–9: Suspect A interviewed via occupational pretext; CLUE #1 (true, unrecognized).
- Ch. 10–11: Suspect B; RED HERRING #1 blooms (the charming newcomer looks guilty).
- Ch. 12–13: The pet-deputy discovery (its one clue-find per book); the law's grudging-respect beat #1.
- Ch. 14: **MIDPOINT: the second body or the near-miss** [attempt on a suspect/witness] — recalibrates the grid; herring #1 collapses.
- Ch. 15–16: Suspect C and D; CLUE #2; a town-secret surfaces (this book's layer — log it in the ledger).
- Ch. 17–18: RED HERRING #2 (the most sympathetic suspect); the romance-ladder milestone if due this book.
- Ch. 19: The sleuth's synthesis scene — her occupation's craft literally assembles the answer [as she blends/bakes/shelves, the pieces click]. She knows — but lacks proof.

**ACT 3 (ch. 20–26)**
- Ch. 20–21: Proof hunt; she tells someone her plan (so the reader can dread it); the law is one step behind by design.
- Ch. 22–23: **Sleuth-in-peril climax (90%):** confronting/confronted by the killer — motive monologued, danger real but brief, self-rescue or last-second arrival. Harm minimal (comfort contract).
- Ch. 24: Fair-play accounting: the reveal walks back through CLUES #1–2 and both herrings — every reader should feel they COULD have solved it.
- Ch. 25: Justice explicit + community restoration [the festival concludes/the shop reopens]; the returning rival's parting shot (series glue).
- Ch. 26: Epilogue: shop life, pet contentment, romance-ladder ember, and the hook for the next event on the town calendar. Recipes/patterns appendix.

**Back matter:** recipe cards → newsletter → series bundle CTA (books 1–3 boxed).

## B3. SERIES TRACKERS
- **Suspect-grid archive** (never reuse a motive-mechanism pair within 5 books)
- **Romance ladder:** meet→respect→spark→first date→interrupted kiss→understanding→engagement — ONE rung per 1–2 books; the ladder is the read-through engine
- **Town-secrets ledger** + **victim tally** (small towns strain credulity — vary venues via the festival calendar, visitors, and neighboring villages by book 6)
- **Fair-play audit** per manuscript: killer introduced in first third ✓ · 2+ true clues ✓ · 2 herrings cashed out ✓ · no outsider-did-it ✓
