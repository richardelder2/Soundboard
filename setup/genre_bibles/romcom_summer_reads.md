## 2. BOOK BEAT SHEET — chapter skeleton (~80k words, ~32 chapters of 2,500 avg, dual POV alternating)

**ACT 1 (ch. 1–8, 0–25%)**
- Ch. 1 (MC): The flameout — fired/jilted/evicted, played for comedy with a bruise under it. Last line: she's going home.
- Ch. 2 (LI): The grump in his element; the shop's threat lands on his counter [notice/letter/offer].
- Ch. 3: **The collision re-meet** — public, embarrassing, set-piece disaster #1 (obligatory: a physical-farce beat by ch. 3).
- Ch. 4–5: She needs [job/lodging]; the only option is his orbit. Terms bickered into place. Running gag #1 established.
- Ch. 6–7: Town chorus assembles around her return; the betting pool opens (the everyone-knew-but-them expectation).
- Ch. 8: Act turn — she commits to help save the shop [her city-skills are exactly what it needs]. Stuck together, stated stakes, festival deadline named.

**ACT 2A (ch. 9–16, 25–50%)**
- Ch. 9–11: Montage-adjacent teamwork chapters: her plans vs. his methods, banter on every spread. Micro-thaw #1 (he does one kind thing and denies it).
- Ch. 12: Vulnerability window — she sees what the shop means to him / he sees the hurt under her polish.
- Ch. 13–14: Set-piece disaster #2 at a town event; they survive it AS A TEAM; the town notices before they do.
- Ch. 15: One-bed / rained-in / tiny-boat beat — farce first, charged silence second.
- Ch. 16: **MIDPOINT: the kiss** (or the almost-that-neither-names). Both privately spiral; running gag #2 escalates.

**ACT 2B (ch. 17–24, 50–75%)**
- Ch. 17–19: Secret-adjacent dating energy; the shop plan gains traction; her old life emails (the city callback SEEDED here, not sprung later).
- Ch. 20–21: Deepening: his grump origin story · her sunshine's cost. Intimacy per heat level.
- Ch. 22–23: The threat counterpunches [developer raises the offer / inspector returns]; a hard choice forms: her comeback opportunity vs. the shop's decisive moment — SAME weekend.
- Ch. 24: False triumph: the festival plan is working; one perfect golden-hour chapter (the screenshot scene).

**ACT 3 (ch. 25–32, 75–100%)**
- Ch. 25–26: **THE CRISIS (kept SHORT — 2 chapters max, this genre resents misery):** a miscommunication-free version preferred — the conflict is the real choice (city vs. cove), not a dumb lie. They part on honesty, not screaming.
- Ch. 27–28: Separation chapters: the city offer in hand and hollow; the shop's final push undermanned. Chorus members deliver truth to each lead (the aunties earn their keep).
- Ch. 29: The refusal — she turns down the callback ON PAGE (her ambition redirected, never erased: she'll build [___] HERE).
- Ch. 30–31: **Festival climax:** the save-the-shop payoff + public declaration with the whole chorus watching + running gags #1 and #2 pay off inside the declaration scene.
- Ch. 32: HEA + epilogue [next summer, the shop thriving, the next book's couple sniping at each other in the background — series hook].

**Back matter:** next book's ch. 1 sample → newsletter magnet (the town's "recipe/guide" PDF) → series page.

## 3. VACATION FLING VARIANT (Fling + He's-the-Boss Reveal + Workplace Rivals, standalone, 75k)
- Act 1 = the fling, rendered idyllically (25% — the "before" IS the fantasy purchase); ends on the collision reveal played for maximum cringe (his face across the Monday conference table).
- Act 2 = rules-of-engagement comedy: deny everything / rival for the same [account/promotion] / proximity keeps reigniting. Midpoint: the fling resumes in secret ("what happens in [island] is apparently a subscription").
- Act 3 crisis = exposure at the worst professional moment; resolution = one of them re-stakes career terms so the love isn't the sacrifice — modern readers require the career NOT be the price.
- Seasonal note: this variant launches April–May only (per Playbook 02 calendar).

## 4. SERIES CONTINUITY TRACKERS
- **Running-gag ledger:** gag → book introduced → escalations → scheduled payoff (gags that pay off across BOOKS create superfans)
- **Chorus promotion pipeline:** which side character headlines next; their spark scenes seeded this book [ch. ___]
- **Town calendar consistency:** festival dates, business names, the group chat's name — readers audit this
- **Banter density check:** open any spread — if no line makes YOU smile, rewrite the spread. This is the genre's quality gate.
