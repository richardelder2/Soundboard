# Hard Sci-Fi Comfort Book Onboarding Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE INTELLECTUAL VIBE
1. Welcome the user to the Hard Sci-Fi brainstorming session. Ask if they are ready to dig into their favorite scientific concepts.
2. "What specific field of science or hard speculative technology absolutely fascinates you right now? (e.g., orbital mechanics, quantum computing, realistic fusion propulsion, terraforming chemistry, deep-space survival microbiology?)"
3. "What is the sensory 'vibe' of the main setting? Describe the machines, the lighting, the sounds, and the atmosphere. Is it a stark, sterile research habitat, or a gritty, lived-in industrial mining ship?"
4. "What are the ambient 'comfort sounds' or visual indicators of safety in this setting? (e.g., the steady pulse of a life-support system, the hum of a particle accelerator, clean green status lights on a console?)"

## STAGE 2: THE COMPETENCE & THE TROPE
5. "What are 2 or 3 of your favorite high-stakes technical tropes? (e.g., Apollo 13-style problem solving, surviving a harsh planetary environment using raw chemistry, deciphering an alien signal using linguistics/math, a complex heist in zero-gravity?)"
6. "What kind of professional dynamic do you love seeing between two highly skilled experts? (e.g., the hyper-precise Pilot and the chaotic-good Lead Engineer, the cynical Astro-Biologist and the idealistic Field Commander?)"
7. "What are some specific 'nerdy micro-elements' you want to self-indulgently include? (e.g., characters writing out equations on glass walls, the precise mechanics of spacesuit maintenance, hacking a localized drone network, drinking terrible synthetic coffee while arguing over telemetry?)"

## STAGE 3: THE RULES OF THE UNIVERSE
8. "Let's establish the hard rules. What is the unyielding physical limitation or environmental law that will act as the 'walls' of this story's puzzle? (e.g., light-speed communication delay, strict reaction-mass limits, extreme radiation zones?)"
9. "How do regular people survive day-to-day under these rules? What does their everyday survival gear or routine look like?"

## STAGE 4: THE CHARACTER BRAIN TRUST
10. "Let's look at Character A (our primary expert). What is their specific scientific or technical discipline, and what is their outward professional persona?"
11. "What is Character A's relatable flaw, mental blindspot, or internal emotional wound? How does their hyper-logical brain try to hide or overcompensate for this flaw?"
12. "Now for Character B (their foil or partner). What is their field of expertise, and how does their way of looking at the universe naturally disrupt or challenge Character A's internal armor?"
13. "What is the immediate first impression or working history these two professionals have with each other?"
14. "What is the exact technical emergency or shared scientific discovery that forces these two to rely on each other's skills completely?"

## STAGE 5: THE EMOTIONAL REALIZATION
15. "When the characters are completely exhausted and up against the wall, what is the 'safe haven' location or ritual on the ship/station where they find temporary relief?"
16. "What is the core theme or emotional takeaway you want to feel at the end of this hard-fought struggle? (e.g., the triumph of human ingenuity, finding meaning in a vast and silent universe, building a family among the stars?)"

## STAGE 6: THE SHAPE OF THE TELLING
These answers set the structural authenticity dials in `_config/narrative_authenticity.md` and feed Stage 02's `structure_plan.md`.
17. "How adventurous should the story's *structure* be? Are you comfortable with flashbacks, time skips, or opening after a disaster and spiraling backward — or do you want mostly linear time with only occasional jumps? (Nonlinear time is one of the strongest markers of human-feeling fiction.)"
18. "How much side-story appetite do you have? Human novels almost always carry subplots — a crewmate's failing marriage, a station politics feud — and not all of them tie neatly into the main theme. Name one or two subplot seeds you'd enjoy, including one allowed to stay messy or unresolved."
19. "Should characters reference the real world by name — actual books, songs, scientists, brands ('she reread her battered copy of *Gravity's Rainbow*') — or should the story world have its own invented named works and brands that characters cite specifically?"

## STAGE 7: SYNTHESIS
20. Review all answers. Synthesize them into a rich, cohesive, mathematically grounded narrative concept summary. Present this back to the user with high enthusiasm and ask: "How does this hard sci-fi blueprint feel? What adjustments should we make to the science or the stakes?"
21. Once approved, craft a final, punchy logline using the classic hook formula.
